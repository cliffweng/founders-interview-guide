# Common traps & weak answers
{: .no_toc }

*~8 min read*

**🎯 Interview frequent**

## Why it matters

Most failed founder interviews are not missing a secret framework. They die on a sentence: a jargon noun, a future-tense plan, a precise ratio built on four customers, or a bad fact tucked under a nicer word. This page is the list partners poke. Read it after the spine, then listen for these in your own mock. See [Mock Q packs](../13-mock-q-packs/).

## Core concepts

- **Marketing language.** "Platform," "AI-powered," "operating system for," "Uber for X" with no mechanic. Seibel's contrast is the right test: "organizes the world's information" tells an outsider nothing; a person, a query, and a list of links does. If a smart friend outside your major can't repeat what you do, rewrite the sentence. See [Product sense](../04-product-sense-prioritization/).
- **All future, no past.** "We will talk to users. We will launch. We will expand." The strong tense is past: we talked to these people, we shipped this, they did this. Future is allowed for the plan. It cannot be the whole answer.
- **TAM theater and fake precision.** A top-down billions number, or "LTV/CAC of 4.7" with no paid acquisition and no lifetime. Ranges you can derive beat decimals you can't. See [Market size](../05-market-size-wedge/) and [Traction](../07-traction-metrics-under-fire/).
- **"No competitors," and the full checkmark matrix.** Both avoid the status quo. Name who they use today. See [Competition](../06-competition-differentiation/).
- **Passion, vision, and the exit slide.** Caring is not evidence. An acquirer is not a strategy. Dalton and Michael's "don't think like an investor" conversation is about this: market maps, exit slides, and a fully worked plan are how founders avoid the user.
- **Hiding the bad fact.** Churn, a cofounder who left, a pilot that didn't convert, a number that includes a one-time fee. Say it in the same breath as the flattering number. The short room finds it by arithmetic. Diligence finds it later, and then the issue is trust.
- **Tarpit ideas with no new fact.** A familiar student idea ("an app to decide Friday night") that hundreds of people have tried. You can still work on a crowded problem. You need a reason this attempt is different, from a user you can describe. "We use AI now" is not that reason.
- **Length.** In a ~10 minute YC interview, a 90-second answer to a 10-second question is how you fail questions they never got to ask. Answer, stop, let them pull. Seed meetings forgive more length and still notice that you can't be concise.

## Mental model

```
1. Plain sentence a non-expert can picture
2. A past-tense fact (user, number, or cut)
3. The ugly qualifier, said by you
4. Stop
```

If your answer skips 2, it is a pitch. If it skips 3, it is a spin. If it skips 4, it is a monologue.

## Interview questions

1. **What's a weak answer to "what do you do?", and the repair?**
   Answer: Weak: "We're building an AI-powered platform that revolutionizes operations for SMBs." Repair: "We turn a clinic's prior-auth PDF into a submitted form. The office manager at a four-doctor practice spent four hours on it last Tuesday. She pays $200 a month." Same company. The second one can be questioned. That's what you want.

2. **You grew 50% last month. What's the weak way to say that, and the strong way?**
   Answer: Weak: "We're growing 50% month over month." Strong: "MRR went from $4k to $6k. $1k of the $6k is a setup fee, so recurring went from $4k to $5k. One of the four customers is a pilot that ends in June." You still grew. You also stayed trustworthy.

3. **They ask about competitors and you say "no one is doing exactly this." What do they hear, and what do you say instead?**
   Answer: They hear that you haven't looked at the workaround. Instead: who the user uses today, where that breaks, and the startup or incumbent they'd mention if you weren't in the room.

4. **What's the tell that a founder is thinking like an investor?**
   Answer: The plan is about the round, the acquirer, or the market map, and the user is abstract. Repair by going back to a person and a last time. The YC conversation on this is explicit: at the stage they fund, the worked-out exit is not what matters. See [Why this / why now / why you](../02-why-this-why-now-why-you/).

5. **You realize mid-answer that you overstated a number. What do you do?**
   Answer: Correct it in the next sentence. "I said ten paying. It's seven paying and three unpaid pilots." Do it before they divide something. Catching yourself is a positive signal. Waiting to be caught is not.
